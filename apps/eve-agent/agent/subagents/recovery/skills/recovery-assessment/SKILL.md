# Recovery assessment

Use this procedure when fatigue, soreness, pain, sleep, illness, or readiness
could change what the user should do next. This is a decision-support process,
not a medical diagnosis.

## Gather the minimum evidence

1. Read `list_workouts` and `list_recovery_checkins` before interpreting the
   user's recent state.
2. Anchor observations to dates. Summarize workout duration, perceived effort,
   spacing, sleep, and soreness trends rather than listing raw records alone.
3. Ask a focused follow-up when the decision depends on missing information:
   location, onset, severity, trend, swelling, range of motion, altered gait,
   or whether ordinary daily activities are affected.
4. Treat missing evidence as uncertainty, not as reassuring evidence.

## Decision zones

Classify the recommendation, not the person:

- **Proceed:** symptoms are mild, improving, do not alter movement, and recent
  load does not suggest an obvious recovery conflict.
- **Modify:** soreness or fatigue is meaningful, sleep is impaired, performance
  is declining, symptoms persist, or hard sessions are tightly clustered.
  Reduce duration, intensity, impact, or density and define a reassessment point.
- **Stop and escalate:** severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, inability to bear
  weight or use the affected area normally, marked swelling/redness/warmth,
  neurological symptoms, chest pain, difficulty breathing, or another urgent
  concern. Recommend appropriate professional or urgent evaluation.

These zones are conservative coaching guidance. Never label an injury or rule
one out.

## Adjustment procedure

1. Identify the near-term decision: train as planned, substitute, reduce, or
   rest.
2. Name the evidence that drives the decision.
3. Preserve safe movement when appropriate, but never frame training through
   worsening pain as necessary for progress.
4. Give a specific replacement session when modifying: activity, duration,
   effort ceiling, and stop conditions.
5. Define the next check-in using observable criteria such as soreness trend,
   normal walking/stairs, sleep, or pain-free range of motion.
6. Separate immediate guidance from the return-to-load progression.

## Recommendation contract

Return a concise specialist report with:

1. **Observed pattern** — dated workload and recovery signals.
2. **Decision zone** — proceed, modify, or stop and escalate, with rationale.
3. **Next-session recommendation** — a concrete action and stop conditions.
4. **Reassessment criteria** — what to observe and when.
5. **Uncertainty** — missing facts that could change the recommendation.

Do not claim that a plan was saved. The parent coach must explain consequential
changes and obtain approval before any application mutation.
